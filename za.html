The reality

55 million people live with dementia today.

And that number is growing faster than any healthcare system can respond. Care is scarce. Attention is rationed. Families are left guessing.

The gap

Most only get a few hours of specialist care per week.

The other 165 hours — patients are without structured support. That's where cognitive decline accelerates unnoticed.

The science

Consistent stimulation slows the progression.

Daily cognitive engagement — memory games, reminiscence, sequencing — can delay decline and improve quality of life measurably.

Our answer

Cognitive Games

We're changing how attention, memory, and focus are trained.

Sequence Reproduction

Let's Play Simon Says

Working-memory sequence training shown to improve attention and memory in older adults with MCI and early Alzheimer's.
Memory

Emoji Tiles

Tile-matching games help form new neural connections and strengthen existing ones — an engaging way to improve recall.
Memory Retention

Time Capsule

A therapeutic reminiscence activity — preserving meaningful items and memories to maintain personal legacy as the disease progresses.
Active Memory

Memory Tray

Objects like keys and coins are placed on a tray, observed briefly, then covered. Patients recall as many items as possible to train short-term memory.
Motor Coordination

Tap Dance Tiles

Rhythmic tap-response exercises that improve motor coordination and reaction timing through musical, movement-based play.
Motor Coordination

Hill Racer Climbing

A physics-based driving game that challenges fine motor control and sustained attention while being genuinely fun to play.
Reaction Speed

Fruit Ninja

Swipe-based slicing game that sharpens hand-eye coordination, reaction speed, and visual tracking in a playful format.
Cognitive Flexibility

Color Swap

A Stroop-inspired challenge where players must override automatic responses, training executive function and mental flexibility.
